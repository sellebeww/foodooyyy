# Backtest report

Generated by `npm run backtest`. Do not edit by hand. Re-running the command reproduces this file exactly.

All data is **simulated**. It is not field evidence.

## Method

- Each seed simulates 12 weeks of a stall with four menus. True demand is drawn from a Poisson distribution with the menu's mean times a weekday factor. Each customer's arrival hour is drawn with probability proportional to the reference busy-hour weight, with a uniform minute inside that hour.
- A menu sells out at the arrival time of customer number cooked + 1. Otherwise leftover = cooked - demand.
- All policies face exactly the same demand and arrival times on every day (common random numbers).
- **Baseline** cooks the usual quantity every day.
- **FOODOO** cooks `suggestQty` using only what the app would have recorded: leftovers, sell-out times and the demand estimate derived from them. It never reads the true demand. It starts with no history, as a new user would, and always cooks exactly its own suggestion.
- **FOODOO without sell-out correction** is a diagnostic, not a product feature. It uses the same rule and inputs, except that a sold-out day is recorded as demand = sales instead of the `estimateDemand` result. Comparing it with FOODOO isolates the effect of the sell-out correction.
- Metrics are summed over weeks 3 to 12 (the first two weeks are warm-up) and over all 3 seeds.
- Lost sales = true demand - cooked, when positive. Profit = sum of min(demand, cooked) x price - sum of cooked x cost. Leftovers have no salvage value.

## Limitations

The data is synthetic. The simulated demand model uses the same busy-hour profile that the estimator assumes, so the estimator is tested on a world that matches its own assumptions; real arrival patterns will differ and the busy-hour profile entered by an owner may be wrong. Demand is Poisson with fixed means, with no trends, no weather or events (days flagged unusual are excluded from suggestions but their demand is not shocked), and no menu-to-menu substitution. The sell-out time is modelled at the arrival of customer cooked + 1, which is slightly later than the moment the last portion is actually sold. The baseline is a fixed usual quantity, not a real owner who adjusts by feel, and the simulated FOODOO owner accepts every suggestion without editing. Leftovers have no salvage value and profit ignores all other costs. Only 3 seeds are used and no confidence intervals are reported. These results show how the rules behave under stated assumptions; they are not evidence of benefit to real stalls. A pilot with real owners is needed for that.
